Three modes, one map

Jump, travel or take the wheel.

Switch modes with ⌘1 ⌘2 ⌘3. They share one map, so a place you starred while teleporting is one click away when you plan a route.

Teleport

⌘1

Jump to an address, a landmark or exact coordinates. Search also reads 48°51'30"N 2°17'40"E and pasted Google or Apple Maps links. Pick a new spot while you're there and press Move Here.

Route

⌘2

Drop a start, a destination and any stops. Follow real walking or driving roads, set a pace from a stroll to highway speed, and play it once, on a loop or back and forth. Pause, scrub and change speed mid-route.

Joystick

⌘3

Steer in real time with the on-screen pad, the arrow keys or W A S D. Up is screen-up, so it follows the map when you rotate it. Hold ⇧ to move 2.5× faster.

Built for long sessions

The details that make it dependable.

Instant moves

The live engine keeps one channel open to the phone, so every move is a single message instead of a fresh connection. That's what makes smooth routes and the joystick possible.

Your real location always comes back

Stop, quit or close the window and the real GPS returns. If the app is ever force-quit, its helper notices and clears the location by itself.

A setup checklist that does the checking

First launch looks for pymobiledevice3, tests the live engine, finds your iPhone and checks Developer Mode, then tells you exactly what to fix.

Favorites, recents and saved routes

Star places, revisit recent ones and keep routes in a library. The command line can use them too, as @Home or @Office.

GPX and KML

Import or export routes, or drop a file on the map. Recorded tracks keep their own timing.

Natural-looking movement

Optional speed variation of up to ±30% and GPS wobble of up to 15 m make routes look less robotic.

Menu bar controls

See where the phone is, pause a route or stop from the menu bar without opening the window.

Control it from your iPhone

The SpoofRemote app finds your Mac over Wi-Fi or your hotspot. Search, tap Start, and the Mac keeps the location alive while it stays in your bag.

Setup

Running in three steps.

1

Connect your iPhone

Plug it in with a cable and tap Trust. After that first pairing, Wi-Fi works too.

2

Turn on Developer Mode

On the iPhone, open Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Developer Mode and restart. The app's checklist tells you if it's off.

3

Pick a place, press ⌘↩

Teleport, start a route or take the joystick. Press ⌘↩ again to stop and restore the real location.

On the iPhoneLocation simulation service

It uses Apple's developer location-simulation service, the same one behind Xcode's Simulate Location. Only latitude and longitude are simulated; iOS works out speed and heading from them, exactly as it does for Xcode.

Two engines

Live when it can, classic when it must.

The live engine runs pymobiledevice3's own command in-process and keeps it open. If your pymobiledevice3 can't support that, the app says so and falls back to the classic engine automatically.

Command line

Prefer the terminal?

iosgpsspoof does everything the app does, from scripts or CI. It understands place names, coordinates, maps links and your saved favorites.

doctor checks pymobiledevice3, the live engine, connected devices and Developer Mode in one go.

zsh
# check everything is ready
$ iosgpsspoof doctor

# hold a place until Ctrl-C, then restore the real GPS
$ iosgpsspoof spoof "Sydney Opera House"
$ iosgpsspoof spoof 48.8584 2.2945

# routes from waypoints or a recorded track
$ iosgpsspoof route @Home @Office --duration 25m --ping-pong
$ iosgpsspoof route ./walk.gpx --loop

Build from source

Four commands on any Mac.

You need Xcode 16 or its command line tools. setup.sh creates a private Python environment with pymobiledevice3 and builds the app and the command line tool.

Prefer Xcode? Run open Package.swift, choose the iosgpsspoofer-gui scheme and press ⌘R.

FAQ

Questions people ask first.

Do I need to jailbreak the iPhone or install anything on it?

No. The app uses a developer service that's built into iOS, the same one Xcode uses. Nothing is installed on the phone.

Do I need a paid Apple Developer account?

No. Developer Mode is free and you turn it on in the iPhone's own Settings app.

How do I get my real location back?

Press Stop (⌘↩), quit the app or restart the iPhone. Any one of them restores the real GPS.

Which iPhones and iOS versions work?

iOS 17 and later connect through Apple's CoreDevice tunnel, and iOS 16 and earlier use the older lockdown service. Both are supported, and the app shows each phone's model and iOS version.

Does it work over Wi-Fi?

Yes, once the phone has been paired with a cable and is on the same network as your Mac.

An app still shows my old location. Why?

Some apps cache your location. Force-quit the app and open it again.

Is this allowed?

It's built for developing and testing location-aware apps on devices you own. Some apps and games forbid location changes in their terms, so check before you use it with them.
